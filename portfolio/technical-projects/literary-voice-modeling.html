Literary Voice Modeling

A modular AI writing system designed to model a distinctive authorial voice without replacing editorial judgment.

Custom AI skill development Prompt architecture Literary style modeling Content workflows

Project overview

A recognizable literary voice depends on more than vocabulary. It emerges through rhythm, syntax, register, narrative stance, rhetorical habits and deliberate choices about what a text should—and should not—claim. I developed and iteratively refined a custom AI writing skill to help reproduce these characteristics across formats, including social media posts, articles, essays and work associated with the literary projects 14 ans et portée disparue and Toxique.

Rather than relying on one long prompt, the system separates core instructions from specialized reference files. This modular structure makes the writing rules easier to maintain, inspect and refine while keeping the author's intent, privacy boundaries and final editorial authority central to the process.

The challenge: making a voice operational

Style is difficult to define as a checklist. The same author may move between analytical precision, lyrical language, self-examination, public advocacy and satire, depending on the subject and intended effect. A useful writing system must recognize these distinct registers without flattening them into a generic tone or applying every stylistic rule to every assignment.

The system also needed boundaries. It had to avoid inventing undocumented personal events, protect anonymity, respect lexical and tonal restrictions, and approach sensitive relational material without reducing complex dynamics to one-sided blame. The goal was not simply to generate text that sounded similar, but to make the intended voice and its editorial principles explicit enough to guide repeatable drafting.

System design: instructions, references and workflow

The documented architecture combines a central skill file with focused reference materials. The central instructions establish the writing posture and workflow; supporting files describe the cardinal rules and the markers that distinguish the voice's registers. This keeps the main instruction set readable while allowing detailed guidance and examples to evolve independently.

Five voice registers

Dissociated clinician, romantic lyricist, self-analyst, public advocate and satirical ventriloquist.

Voice markers

Register-specific openings, syntax, vocabulary, rhetorical patterns and closing forms.

Editorial constraints

Anonymity, anti-extrapolation, lexical restrictions and analysis of relational dynamics rather than public blame.

Structured production

A documented eight-block template for Facebook posts, with a register choice, defined drafting structure, engagement elements and visual guidance.

Self-check guidance

A review protocol intended to help detect departures from the brief and the established voice before human review.

Instruction hierarchy and authorial control

The system is designed to support a recognizable voice without turning a style guide into an inflexible rulebook. The author's current instructions take precedence over standing skill instructions and reference guidelines. That hierarchy allows a specific assignment to depart from habitual conventions when the subject, audience or intended effect calls for it.

Human editorial control is intentional. The system assists with drafting and consistency; it does not decide whether a text is faithful to the author's intentions or appropriate to publish. This distinction is especially important when the writing addresses personal experiences or sensitive subjects.

Illustrative use and iterative evaluation

The skill is documented as version 3 and has been refined through repeated use. Tests have included social media posts and draft chapters for Toxique. In one documented example, a Facebook post was reported as final and published after its first generation, without additional revision. Project notes also describe outputs as qualitatively stronger than generation without the skill, particularly in terms of reducing manual corrections.

These observations are practical feedback, not the result of a controlled benchmark. No standardized evaluation set, measured improvement percentage or independently verified success rate is available. The example demonstrates a successful use case; it should not be read as a guarantee that future outputs will require no editing.

Scope and limitations

Literary Voice Modeling is a custom instruction and reference system for AI-assisted writing, not a separately trained language model. Its behavior depends on how the instructions and reference materials are supplied and interpreted by the model in use. The documented self-check supports review but is not fully automated quality assurance, and it does not replace the author's judgment.

The project is intended to help structure and guide drafting, not to guarantee perfect voice replication, factual accuracy or publication readiness. Outputs remain subject to review, particularly when they include sensitive personal material. No causal link is claimed between the skill and the reported publishing reach.

What the project demonstrates

  • Translating qualitative stylistic traits into explicit, reusable instructions.
  • Designing modular prompt architecture and reference materials.
  • Differentiating multiple registers within one authorial voice.
  • Encoding editorial constraints, privacy boundaries and anti-extrapolation rules.
  • Building a structured content-production workflow with human review.
  • Iteratively refining an AI-assisted writing process through practical use and qualitative feedback.
The bottom line!

The takeaway

This project turns a distinctive literary voice into a structured, reusable set of instructions and references—making AI-assisted drafting more deliberate while preserving the author's control over meaning, boundaries and publication.
