Caifan Accounting System Implementation and Operations Automation

Accounting systems Time-tracking workflow Payroll preparation Daily sales accounting

Project overview

At Restaurant Caifan, I implemented QuickBooks Online from paper-based accounting records and connected recurring operational routines through low-code automation. The work brought together accounting setup, employee time tracking, payroll preparation, and the handling of daily sales reports.

Rather than treating each task as a separate administrative activity, I designed connected workflows around the information each process needed to capture, review, and pass on. The resulting setup combined accounting software with time-tracking, spreadsheets, email, cloud storage, and automation tools.

The challenge: connecting routine work

The project involved several recurring processes with different source materials and review needs: historical accounting information had to be reconstructed from paper records; employee hours needed to be collected and checked before payroll preparation; and daily sales reports arriving by email had to be retained, interpreted, and translated into accounting entries.

The practical objective was to establish repeatable processes and reduce the need to handle the same information manually at multiple stages, while preserving opportunities for review where employee or accounting confirmation was required.

Accounting setup

I implemented QuickBooks Online using the available paper records, reconstructed the year-to-date accounting history, and established recurring accounting processes. This created the accounting foundation for the connected workflows.

Connected workflows

1

QR-based employee time tracking

I designed an employee time-tracking workflow using employee-specific QR codes, a shared Android tablet, Clockify, and Make. The setup provided a common point for employees to record time while associating entries with individual employees.

2

Biweekly review before payroll preparation

The workflow generated biweekly time reports in Google Sheets. Employees could review and confirm their reported hours before the information was used for payroll processing. This review step was part of the process rather than an assumption that automated time records were always correct.

3

Daily sales-report processing

I set up a workflow to monitor daily sales-report emails, archive the source reports in Google Drive, extract sales, tax, and tip figures, and prepare corresponding journal entries in QuickBooks Online. The source reports remained available for reference.

The documented scope supports describing the workflow as preparing journal entries; it does not establish that every entry was automatically posted to the accounting ledger without review.

Operational use and constraints

The system and connected workflows were used operationally for approximately two years. Recurring software costs were about CAD $10 per month, excluding development and implementation costs.

No quantified time savings, error-reduction rate, or other performance metric is available. The project is therefore best described through its implemented processes and operational use, rather than through unmeasured efficiency claims.

What the project demonstrates

  • Implementing an accounting system and reconstructing year-to-date records from paper-based source material.
  • Connecting time tracking, employee review, and payroll preparation through a low-code workflow.
  • Using Make to link operational tools and move information between process stages.
  • Structuring daily sales-report handling, source-document archiving, data extraction, and journal-entry preparation.
  • Combining accounting knowledge with workflow design to make recurring processes more consistent and reviewable.
The bottom line!

The takeaway

Accounting knowledge and low-code integration can turn fragmented manual routines into structured, reviewable workflows—from record reconstruction and employee time tracking to daily sales accounting.
