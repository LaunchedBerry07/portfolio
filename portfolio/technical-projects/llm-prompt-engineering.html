LLM Prompt Engineering & Logic Routing

Advanced instruction architecture and conditional analysis pipelines for complex literary data.

Instruction architecture Conditional routing Source-grounded analysis NotebookLM

Project overview

Complex analytical work rarely fits one reusable prompt. Within the literary project Toxique: L’Ossature des Ruines, different questions call for different structures: a character profile, a relationship timeline, a strategic analysis, or a record of specific events. The right format also depends on the relationship category assigned to the character within the project.

I designed a structured instruction architecture for NotebookLM that connects these decisions. It first checks a character against a master matrix, then uses the resulting project-defined category to route the request to a relevant task module and reporting template. The aim is to make complex outputs more consistent, traceable to source material, and responsive to the specific analytical task—not to make the model’s interpretation automatically correct.

The challenge: one project, different analytical needs

A single generic instruction can produce summaries that are too broad or too accommodating for a task that requires a particular structure, level of detail, or evidentiary standard. The workflow needed to account for both what was being analysed and what kind of document the request required. It also needed to reduce unsupported extrapolation when working with a large collection of personal notes and narrative source material.

The practical challenge was designing logic that adapts dynamically without requiring complete manual rewrites for every analytical variation.

From classification to task-specific output

The architecture separates the decision process into stages. First, the character is looked up in a master matrix to identify the relationship category defined for the project: Toxique, Sécure, or Miroir. The category then informs which task-specific template or version should be used.

Master-matrix lookup

Classifies the entity into structural relationship categories prior to generation.

Conditional request modules

Encompasses specialized profiles, strategic analysis reports, relationship timelines, meeting logs, and narrative transition sheets.

Specialized reporting templates

Matches the structure of the output to the precise task and category rather than relying on a monolithic format.

Keeping the analysis tied to its sources

The instructions define how the model should use the supplied material. When a template calls for verbatim evidence, the output must include exact quotations from the source documents. Sections marked by explicit directives must be addressed, and the analytical framing must follow the relevant project category. These constraints are designed to make omissions and unsupported interpretations easier to spot during review.

The architecture also discourages generic, overly accommodating summaries. Its purpose is to organize the analysis and make its evidentiary basis more visible—not to treat an LLM’s output as an authoritative account of events or people.

Iteration and limitations

Practical use showed that some outputs did not consistently reach the required depth, so the template instructions were refined. The project records do not provide a controlled benchmark, a quantified improvement rate, or a formal evaluation of routing accuracy. The workflow is an applied literary and analytical tool, not a clinically validated diagnostic system. Its categories are project-specific working labels and should not be interpreted as clinical classifications.

What the project demonstrates

  • Designing multi-stage instructions that separate classification, task selection, and output generation.
  • Translating complex analytical requirements into conditional routing and reusable templates.
  • Defining source-grounded requirements, including verbatim quotation when requested and explicit coverage of required sections.
  • Refining instructions in response to qualitative review of actual outputs.
  • Recognizing the limits of LLM-generated analysis and the need for human interpretation and verification.
The bottom line!

The takeaway

Reliable prompt engineering is not just a matter of writing a longer instruction. It means structuring the decisions around a task—what to look up, which analytical frame to apply, what evidence to include, and how the result should be organized—while keeping the limits of the model’s conclusions visible.
