Ford Build & Price

Localizing three complete vehicle-configuration journeys from US English into Canadian French, with attention to automotive terminology, pricing information and interface constraints.

Automotive localization Interactive experience 50,284 words US English → Canadian French

Project details

  • Language Direction US English Canadian French
  • Scope 50,284 words localized
  • Delivery 3 batches
  • Client Relationship Ford assignment via TransPerfect

About the Client

An American automotive manufacturer

Ford Motor Company is an American multinational automotive manufacturer headquartered in Dearborn, Michigan. Founded in 1903, the company designs, manufactures and distributes cars, trucks, SUVs and commercial vehicles under the Ford and Lincoln brands. It also develops electric vehicles, connected technologies and mobility solutions, serving customers in markets around the world.

Project overview

A connected digital journey

Build & Price brings together vehicle filters, configuration choices, model specifications, starting prices and legal disclosures. The language across these elements needs to remain consistent while helping Canadian French-speaking users understand the information presented at each step.

Across three assignments, I localized three complete vehicle-configuration journeys, covering a combined 50,284 words delivered in three batches. The work required attention to terminology, readability and the way individual pieces of content fit into the wider interface.

The Challenge

Keeping the experience coherent from screen to screen

A vehicle configurator is a connected decision-making experience. A term introduced during model selection may reappear in a configuration choice, an option description or a summary. Inconsistent wording can make relationships between vehicle features and available choices harder to follow. The localization therefore called for more than sentence-level accuracy: terminology, readability, interface constraints and continuity had to be considered together.

Key considerations included:

  • Maintaining consistent automotive terminology across vehicle information, attributes and configuration options.
  • Keeping labels and descriptions aligned across successive stages of the journey.
  • Distinguishing clearly between vehicle features, equipment, packages and selectable options where presented.
  • Adapting commercial and pricing content for a Canadian French-speaking audience.
  • Keeping legal disclosures concise and readable within space-constrained interface modals.
  • Preserving continuity across three assignments and their separate delivery batches.

Scope of Work

Localization with the interface in mind

I translated and localized the interactive Ford Build & Price experience from US English into Canadian French. The three assignments each covered a complete vehicle-configuration journey. The content included the sequence of choices involved in configuring a vehicle, with the exact options varying according to the model.

The main content areas included:

  • Vehicle filters, model information and specifications.
  • Vehicle style and configuration choices.
  • Exterior colour and powertrain options.
  • Packages, equipment and feature descriptions.
  • Exterior and interior features, accessories and related configuration details.
  • Starting-price content and legal disclosures, including information presented in compact pricing modals.

The sequence above describes the types of content involved; it is not intended as a claim that every vehicle model offered an identical set of options or screens.

Linguistic framework & workflow

Localizing within a defined language system

The work combined Ford-specific Canadian French conventions with the practical constraints of a contextualized localization environment.

01 / Linguistic framework

Applying Ford-specific guidance in Canadian French

Language conventions

The localization was governed by a French (Canada) language style guide setting out conventions for tone, product names and trademarks, calls to action, capitalization, punctuation, numbers, dates, time, currency, acronyms and units of measurement. These rules provided a consistent linguistic framework for adapting Ford content while respecting the distinction between brand terminology and text intended for localization.

This meant making context-sensitive decisions rather than applying mechanical word-for-word substitutions. Product and trademark names had to be preserved according to the guide, while accompanying descriptors and general interface content were adapted to Canadian French conventions. Pricing and measurement content also required care because some values appeared as system-managed elements.

02 / Localization environment

Considering source context, tags and dynamic content

Context and constraints

The project used the OneLink localization environment, which provided access to source content in context and an online preview of the translation. The files contained tags and tokenized elements that had to be preserved and handled according to the project workflow. Some values, such as numbers and measurements, were system-managed; the linguistic work therefore had to respect the limits of what could be adapted within the files.

The OneLink guidance also required attention to segmentation and source integrity: linguists were instructed not to edit source text or split and merge segments, and to refer source issues to the project team. The work involved localizing content within the supplied environment, not modifying the underlying website or its technical implementation.

03 / Terminology & quality checks

Using project references and prescribed verification steps

References and verification

Project instructions called for consultation of the style guide and glossary, use of the relevant translation resources, and a Transcheck run before delivery. The supplied project reports also document SDLXLIFF-based analysis and translation activity across multiple files and batches, including the use of Ford/Lincoln translation memories.

Together, these resources supported consistent terminology and repeatable handling of recurring content. They also helped frame quality control around the project’s actual requirements: preserving tags, respecting source-file structure, following the language guidance and checking the files using the prescribed workflow.

Sentence-level accuracy is not enough

What the assignment required

The central task was to make each localized element work both independently and as part of a connected customer journey. That required automotive terminology, clear commercial wording, Canadian French conventions and awareness of the interface context. Pricing and legal disclosures called for particular precision, while the separate batches made consistency across related content especially important.

A localized interface must function as a coherent journey—not as a series of independently translated segments.
