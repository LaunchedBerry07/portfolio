About the Client

A federal organization supporting government-wide services

Public Works / Government Services Canada (GSC) is a federal government organization responsible for providing common services and support to Canadian federal departments and agencies. Its activities include procurement, real property management, project delivery and government-wide administrative services. The organization serves federal departments and agencies across Canada, supporting the delivery of public services and the day-to-day operations of the federal government.

Project overview

Translating institutional content with terminological precision

This body of work brought together 14 institutional translation assignments totalling 101,450 words. The assignments were completed separately, but they shared a public-sector context and a common language direction: Canadian English into Canadian French.

Within this group, one core project accounted for 43,521 words, representing a substantial portion of the overall volume. The assignments required me to work with institutional content where terminology, register and linguistic consistency were particularly important.

My role was to translate the supplied English content into clear, accurate Canadian French while respecting the conventions expected in government-related communications. This meant paying attention not only to the meaning of individual expressions, but also to the terminology used in an institutional environment.

Working across multiple assignments also meant keeping my linguistic decisions consistent from one delivery to another, while remaining attentive to the context of each text.

Institutional translation

Three Priorities in Institutional Translation

The assignments called for attention to official terminology, administrative language and consistency across separate deliveries.

01 / Official terminology

Choosing established government terminology

Terminological accuracy

Government terminology requires particular care. In institutional content, a term may have an established French equivalent that differs from a more general or commonly used translation.

I therefore consulted TERMIUM Plus, the Government of Canada's terminology and linguistic data bank, to research and verify specialized terminology. This resource provided a reference point for identifying appropriate French equivalents and checking their alignment with established government usage.

Rather than relying exclusively on general language intuition, I used terminology research to support my choices and make sure that the wording reflected the institutional context.

This was especially important when dealing with administrative concepts for which precision and consistency matter.

02 / Administrative register

Adapting language to an institutional setting

Register and readability

Translating public-sector content also involves adapting the language to its intended institutional setting.

I paid particular attention to the administrative register, the level of formality and the clarity of the French wording. The goal was to produce translations that read naturally in Canadian French while retaining the meaning and intent of the English source.

This required balancing accuracy with readability. A translation can be technically correct yet feel awkward or overly literal. My approach was to look for formulations that conveyed the original information clearly while remaining appropriate to the conventions of French-language institutional communication.

03 / Consistency

Maintaining coherence across separate assignments

Cross-assignment continuity

The 14 assignments were delivered as separate pieces of work, rather than as one continuous document. Nevertheless, the shared institutional context made consistency an important consideration.

I approached terminology and editorial choices with this broader context in mind. When a concept or expression recurred, I considered the terminology already established and the need to maintain a coherent vocabulary across related deliveries.

At the same time, consistency did not mean applying the same wording mechanically in every situation. Each term still needed to be appropriate to its immediate context and intended meaning.

My Terminology Research Approach

Using authoritative references to support translation decisions

TERMIUM Plus was an important reference in this assignment. As a terminology resource maintained by the Government of Canada, it offered a way to investigate specialized expressions and compare possible equivalents within a Canadian institutional context.

My terminology research focused on three complementary considerations:

  • Terminological accuracy: identifying French equivalents that accurately conveyed the concepts expressed in the English source.
  • Institutional usage: checking whether a proposed term aligned with established Government of Canada terminology and language conventions.
  • Consistency: keeping terminology choices coherent across the different assignments whenever the context allowed it.

I treated terminology research as an integral part of the translation process, rather than as a final check performed only after the text had been translated. Consulting reliable references helped inform my decisions and supported a more consistent approach to institutional language.

TERMIUM Plus served as a terminology reference; this does not imply that the translations were approved or certified by the resource.

What This Work Required

Accuracy, research and attention to institutional context

This project brought together several aspects of my translation practice: careful reading of the source, research into specialized terminology, sensitivity to administrative language and attention to consistency.

I had to consider the intended meaning of each passage while selecting French formulations suited to the institutional context. Terminology research through TERMIUM Plus supported this process, particularly when dealing with concepts requiring established government equivalents.

The work also reinforced the importance of treating each assignment as part of a broader linguistic environment. Even when documents are translated and delivered separately, terminology and editorial decisions contribute to the coherence of the overall body of content.

Key Takeaway

Institutional translation is not simply a matter of replacing words from one language with words from another. It requires an understanding of context, careful terminology research and the ability to produce clear, appropriate language while maintaining consistency across separate assignments.

Through these Public Works / GSC assignments, I applied that approach to a substantial volume of Canadian English content, using Government of Canada terminology resources to support precise and consistent Canadian French translations.
