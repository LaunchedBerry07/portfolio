About the Client

A Japanese manufacturer of precision equipment

NAKANISHI INC. is a Japanese manufacturer specializing in dental, surgical and industrial equipment. Founded in 1930 and headquartered in Tochigi, Japan, the company develops precision instruments and technologies for dental professionals, medical applications and industrial manufacturing. Its products are distributed internationally, supporting healthcare professionals and specialized industries worldwide.

Project overview

Technical translation for a dental surgical system

I translated the English-language operation manual for the Surgic Pro2, a dental surgical micromotor system, into Canadian French. The assignment involved 14,699 words across a 65-file translation package.

The manual is intended for qualified dental professionals, including dentists and oral surgeons. It introduces the system's main components, including the control unit, micromotor, handpiece, irrigation system and foot control, then explains how to prepare, operate, maintain and troubleshoot the equipment.

This range of information makes the manual a connected body of technical documentation. The same component may appear in a package list, a setup instruction, a control-panel description or a specification table. I paid particular attention to keeping terminology recognizable and consistent across these different contexts so that readers could clearly identify the parts and understand how they relate to one another.

The content also combines concise lists and technical tables with detailed, numbered procedures. My translation needed to preserve these relationships while producing natural, readable Canadian French. Each instruction had to remain clear on its own and retain its connection to the relevant heading, warning, diagram or specification.

The challenge

Preserving technical distinctions

The main linguistic challenge was to preserve technical distinctions while making the instructions clear and practical for Canadian French-speaking professionals.

The manual differentiates between several interconnected components: the control unit, micromotor, handpiece, irrigation tubing and foot control. It also explains how these components connect, how operating settings are selected and how calibration supports the system's torque readings. I maintained consistent terminology throughout to help readers connect each instruction to the correct component.

Safety information required an equally careful approach. Warnings, cautions and notes communicate different levels and types of information, while setup instructions and operating procedures depend on specific conditions and sequences. I preserved these distinctions, along with the source's units, model identifiers, operating limits and prescribed order of actions.

Interface terminology

Clear labels. Precise functions.

The control-panel terminology presented another challenge. The manual describes a touch display with program, speed and torque settings, as well as foot-control functions for coolant flow, program selection, forward and reverse rotation, and speed adjustment.

Because interface labels are necessarily concise, I had to ensure that their French equivalents remained distinct, understandable and consistent with the functions described in the documentation.

Technical context

Components, procedures and specifications

The manual covers both product information and practical operating procedures. Its content includes installation, control-panel functions, calibration, wireless foot-control settings, irrigation, connections to external terminals, cleaning, sterilization and troubleshooting.

Technical specifications

I paid close attention to keeping every value associated with the correct component, measurement and unit. In technical documentation, numerical accuracy and the relationships between specifications are just as important as terminology.

  • Torque range5–80 N·cm
  • Motor speed range200–40,000 min⁻¹
  • Maximum irrigation-pump output75 mL/min

Connected equipment and functions

The documentation also describes a colour touch panel, an irrigation pump, wireless connectivity and connections with an iPad or the Osseo 100+ implant-stability device.

These features bring together physical components, interface labels, operating settings and connected equipment. I focused on conveying these relationships clearly without introducing explanations or claims beyond the source content.

Translation workflow

Working across structured files and formats

I managed the translation in SDL Trados Studio. The project package contained 65 files, including DITA sections and separate IDML files for the motor and foot control.

Because the content was distributed across multiple files and formats, maintaining consistent terminology and document relationships was particularly important. I worked with the structured content while ensuring that component names, procedural steps, tables and interface references remained associated with the appropriate information.

Component identification

The manual also identifies components by both model number and function. For example, it lists control unit NE335, micromotors SGL80M and SG80M, and wireless foot control FC-81.

I took care to preserve these identifiers accurately and distinguish individual components from the complete Surgic Pro2 system. Working with structured technical files requires attention not only to the wording itself, but also to the organization and relationships that give the documentation its practical meaning.
