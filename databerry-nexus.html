Technical project · Google Workspace

DataBerry Nexus

Business correspondence often contains more than a message: it may include invoices, supporting documents, sender details and information that needs to be filed or reviewed later. When these elements remain scattered across email, folders and spreadsheets, it becomes harder to maintain a clear, traceable record of what has been processed.

DataBerry Nexus is an independent Google Workspace application designed to connect email categorization, document archiving and structured contact information in one workflow. Built with Google Apps Script and a browser-based interface, it brings together Gmail, Google Drive and Google Sheets while retaining human review for messages that require a decision.

The challenge: connecting separate steps

Email processing is not just a matter of sorting messages. A useful workflow also needs to preserve the relationship between a conversation, its archived document and the information recorded for later retrieval. Classification rules must be adjustable, and ambiguous cases should not automatically be treated as settled decisions.

The project explores how these steps can be connected without treating every email as identical or assuming that automated classification is always correct.

From inbox to structured records

Google Apps Script provides the application logic, while HTML, CSS and JavaScript support the web interface. Gmail is the source of correspondence; Google Drive stores generated documents in an organized folder structure; and Google Sheets holds structured records, including contact information.

The documented workflow brings together several components:

  • Email status labels. Gmail labels distinguish conversations awaiting processing (Nexus/To-Process) from those marked as processed (Nexus/Processed).
  • Organized document storage. A Drive folder hierarchy separates items to process from processed items, with year- and month-based organization.
  • PDF generation and archiving. The application has generated PDF documents from email content and archived them in Drive. Demonstrated examples include utility invoices and cloud-service billing statements.
  • Contact register. A Google Sheets register stores names or organizations, email addresses and timestamps.
  • Rule and record management. The interface includes components for managing rules and records, as well as CSV export. The integrity and synchronization of all views still require validation.
  • Human confirmation. Email categorization and confirmation components support review of items where a person needs to verify the proposed handling.

Why the approach matters

DataBerry Nexus treats email handling as a connected information workflow rather than a collection of isolated actions. The design links classification, document creation, archiving and contact records, making the process easier to inspect and potentially easier to maintain. Human confirmation remains part of the model because message context can be ambiguous.

The project also brings together process analysis and implementation: identifying the information that needs to move between tools, defining a workable structure, and building an interface around those operations.

Scope, status and limitations

DataBerry Nexus is a functional application under refinement; end-to-end validation is still pending. Email categorization, human-confirmation components, PDF generation and Drive archiving have been demonstrated, but the complete sequence has not yet been validated as a reliable end-to-end system.

The contact register and interface components exist, but synchronization and data integrity across all views still need testing. Dashboard figures are illustrative rather than verified operational metrics. Contact-based suggestions for new rules and automated extraction of financial fields are planned directions, not established capabilities of the current implementation.

The project therefore demonstrates a working set of components and an evolving workflow design—not a fully validated, production-ready product. No measured processing accuracy, time savings or business outcomes are claimed.

What the project demonstrates

  • Connecting Gmail, Google Drive and Google Sheets through Google Apps Script.
  • Designing a workflow that links email categorization, document archiving and structured records.
  • Generating PDF documents from email content and organizing them in Drive.
  • Building browser-based interface components for rule and record management.
  • Including human review where automated categorization may require confirmation.
  • Distinguishing demonstrated functionality from planned features and unvalidated end-to-end behavior.

DataBerry Nexus explores how everyday email can become a more structured, reviewable information workflow by connecting classification, document archiving and contact data—while keeping the limits of automation visible.
