About the Client

A regional organization within the Seventh-day Adventist Church

The Fédération adventiste du Québec is the regional administrative organization of the Seventh-day Adventist Church in Quebec, Canada. It is part of the wider Seventh-day Adventist Church in Canada, a Protestant Christian denomination with congregations and ministries across the country. The federation supports the Adventist community in Quebec, whose faith and teachings are rooted in biblical principles and the beliefs of the worldwide church.

Project overview

Translating a complete book for a French-speaking readership

As part of my work with Services administratifs Chantale Nadeau, I was assigned the translation of Could It Be This Simple? A Biblical Model for Healing the Mind into Canadian French. The project covered 62,196 words and involved a complete book-length text intended for members of the church community.

Unlike shorter editorial assignments, translating a full-length book requires sustained attention to the relationship between chapters, recurring concepts and the author's overall voice. A terminology choice made early in the text can influence how a concept is expressed many chapters later. I therefore approached the work as a continuous editorial project rather than as a series of independent passages.

The subject matter also called for particular care. The book addresses themes including mental health, guilt, forgiveness, interpersonal relationships and personal restoration within a spiritual and pastoral framework. My task was to convey these ideas clearly in Canadian French while remaining attentive to the tone and intent of the source text.

The Challenge

Preserving voice, meaning and sensitivity across a long text

The challenge was not simply the volume of material. It was maintaining a coherent reading experience throughout an extensive work dealing with personal and sensitive subjects.

The language needed to remain accessible and empathetic without flattening the author's voice or changing the meaning of concepts presented in a religious context. Expressions related to emotional well-being, personal responsibility, forgiveness and faith can carry different nuances depending on their context. I had to consider those nuances while making sure that recurring ideas remained recognizable throughout the book.

My priorities included:

  • Narrative continuity: maintaining a consistent voice and register from one chapter to another.
  • Terminological consistency: selecting appropriate French equivalents for recurring concepts and using them consistently.
  • Tone and sensitivity: choosing language that remained respectful and empathetic when addressing personal experiences and mental health.
  • Readability: producing natural Canadian French that could be followed comfortably by the intended readership.
  • Conceptual accuracy: preserving the distinctions and relationships between ideas rather than relying on literal sentence-by-sentence equivalence.

My Approach

Creating dedicated translation memories

I created two translation memories specifically for this assignment rather than relying on resources supplied by the client. The project used Adventiste.sdltm and CouldItBeThisSimple_Adventiste_Adventiste.sdltm as translation resources in SDL Trados.

Creating project-specific memories allowed me to build a reusable record of translation segments and the linguistic decisions made throughout the assignment. In a book-length project, this is particularly useful when similar passages, recurring expressions or previously translated formulations appear again. Instead of approaching each occurrence in isolation, I could draw on the work already completed and maintain continuity across the text.

The SDL Trados analysis identified 854 repeated segments, representing 1,205 words. This gives an indication of the recurring material within the source document and helps explain why dedicated translation resources were relevant to the assignment.

These memories were my own working resources, created for this mandate. They were not supplied by the client, and their development formed part of the way I organized and supported my translation work.

Terminology management

Keeping recurring concepts clear and consistent

Translation memories and terminology management serve related but different purposes. A translation memory retains previously translated segments, while terminology management focuses on the words and expressions used to represent specific concepts.

Translation memory

Reusing established translation decisions

The two project-specific memories provided a way to retain translated segments and refer back to previous formulations when similar or identical content recurred. They supported continuity across a long text without replacing the need to assess each passage in context.

Terminology

Managing meaning across recurring themes

I paid particular attention to recurring vocabulary associated with mental health, emotional experiences, relationships, forgiveness, faith and spiritual restoration. I considered terminology in context rather than treating individual words as isolated equivalents. The same expression can require different treatment depending on whether it appears in an explanation, a personal reflection or a passage offering guidance.

My objective was to avoid unnecessary variation in the French terminology while preserving the distinctions the author made between related concepts. This was an important part of maintaining continuity across a text of more than 62,000 words.

Editorial considerations

Balancing clarity, tone and terminology

The book's subject matter required more than consistent vocabulary. The translation also had to preserve the relationship between the author's explanations, reflections and guidance.

When translating passages about mental health and personal relationships, wording can influence how readers interpret an idea or perceive its tone. I paid particular attention to avoiding formulations that might sound unnecessarily harsh, detached or overly clinical when the source called for an empathetic approach.

The religious context added another layer of consideration. Concepts rooted in biblical principles and spiritual beliefs needed to remain clear and coherent in French, without losing their place within the author's broader message.

I treated terminology, tone and context as interconnected elements of the translation rather than as separate concerns.

What the Assignment Required

Editorial consistency from beginning to end

This assignment brought together the main challenges of long-form editorial translation: a substantial word count, recurring concepts, sensitive subject matter and the need for a consistent voice across an entire book.

I translated 62,196 words from English into Canadian French and created two dedicated translation memories to support my work. I also had to manage recurring terminology and maintain consistent linguistic choices while remaining attentive to readability, context and the author's intended tone.

The French translation was intended for members of the church community. This case study describes the translation assignment; it does not imply that the French text was commercially published as a separate edition.

Key takeaway

“Accuracy is not only a matter of finding the right words. It also means developing reliable linguistic resources, making thoughtful terminology decisions and sustaining the same care, voice and editorial judgment across the entire work.”
