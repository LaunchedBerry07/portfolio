About the Client

A Canadian media and entertainment company

Bell Media is a Canadian media and entertainment company with a portfolio spanning television, radio, digital media, audio and out-of-home advertising. Its brands include CTV, Crave, TSN, RDS and Noovo. Bell Media is part of BCE Inc., one of Canada's largest communications companies.

Project overview

Making operational instructions clear and usable

This project involved the post-editing of operational content used by Bell Media call-centre agents. The materials included structured workflows, referred to as Blueprints, designed to guide agents through specific customer-service situations.

Rather than presenting information as continuous prose, these workflows organized instructions into interconnected steps and decision paths. Each Blueprint addressed a particular type of request or issue, such as a billing question or a technical troubleshooting situation. Agents relied on these visual flows to determine what action to take next during a customer interaction.

The assignment required linguistic work within this structured environment. Instructions had to remain clear, concise and consistent while preserving the logic and sequence of the original workflows.

The project comprised a total of 16,293 words of English-to-Canadian French content, primarily involving full machine translation post-editing, alongside a separately recorded translation component.

The objective was to produce French-language content that agents could understand and apply efficiently while respecting the project’s terminology, formatting and interface requirements.

The Challenge

Preserving meaning within a visual workflow

The main challenge was to preserve the operational logic of content embedded in visual workflows. Unlike conventional documents, these materials could not be reviewed as isolated paragraphs or independent sentences. Each instruction had to make sense within its specific position in the flow and in relation to the decisions and actions surrounding it. Several production constraints added complexity to the linguistic work.

Key considerations included:

  • Structured visual content: instructions and labels had to remain consistent with the sequence and logic of the Visio workflows.
  • Operational clarity: wording needed to be immediately understandable and actionable in a live call-centre environment.
  • Terminology compliance: predefined glossaries and project-specific terminology requirements had to be followed consistently.
  • Interface limitations: character limits, formatting rules and restrictions on specific characters affected the available wording.
  • Source–target consistency: the English and French content had to remain aligned throughout the workflow files.

Scope of Work

Post-editing across operational content

The assignment covered operational flows and related content, with machine translation post-editing as the principal activity. A separate translation component was also included in the documented project volume. The work therefore required more than correcting grammatical or stylistic issues: every linguistic decision had to account for the practical environment in which the translated content would be used.

The assignment involved:

  • Operational workflows: post-editing instructions and decision paths contained in Visio Blueprints.
  • Related content: working with associated text and reference materials supplied for the assignment.
  • Linguistic quality assurance: checking terminology, formatting, character limits, forbidden characters and source–target consistency.

Linguistic framework & workflow

A structured approach to operational language

The post-editing process combined contextual review, terminology control and targeted quality checks to ensure that the French content met the project's linguistic and production requirements.

01 / Context

Understand the workflow

The Visio Blueprints were essential references throughout the assignment. Their visual structure provided context that could not always be understood from individual text segments alone. Each instruction was considered in relation to its place in the workflow, helping preserve the intended sequence and distinctions between different customer-service situations.

  • Meaning and intent
  • Sequence and decision paths
  • Consistent headings and labels

02 / Requirements

Apply terminology and constraints

The project relied on predefined terminology resources, including Bell-specific glossaries and supplementary reference files. The production instructions also imposed specific restrictions on the French text, including character limits, formatting conventions, forbidden characters and blacklisted terms. A linguistically correct formulation was not necessarily suitable if it exceeded a limit or failed to match the target environment.

  • Glossary compliance
  • Character and formatting limits
  • Clear, concise formulations

03 / Quality assurance

Verify consistency

Wordfast Transcheck was used to perform targeted checks against the project’s requirements. The verification process included terminology compliance, blacklisted terms, forbidden characters, character limits, source consistency and target consistency. The visual source files remained important references during quality assurance, allowing the translated content to be checked against the structure and context of the original workflows.

  • Terminology and blacklist checks
  • Forbidden-character and character-limit checks
  • Source and target consistency
Language in action

What the assignment required

Linguistic accuracy, operational clarity and technical compliance

This assignment illustrates the particular demands of post-editing operational content. The work had to satisfy several requirements simultaneously: linguistic accuracy, terminology consistency, structural coherence, interface compatibility and immediate usability.

The challenge was not simply to make machine-translated text sound natural. It was to ensure that the resulting French instructions functioned within a predefined process and supported agents as they navigated customer interactions.

Key takeaway

“In an operational workflow, a sentence must do more than read well: it must fit the process, respect its constraints and help the user take the right next step.”
