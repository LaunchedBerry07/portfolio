About the Client

A U.S. financial services institution

U.S. Bank is a financial services institution operating in the United States. This assignment involved reviewing French-language content associated with the brand, with particular attention to established project terminology and brand-specific conventions.

Project overview

Contextual review guided by project resources

In February 2018, I completed a linguistic revision and contextual proofreading assignment for U.S. Bank, covering 1,500 words translated from US English into Canadian French.

Although relatively short, the assignment required more than checking individual sentences for grammatical accuracy. The available project instructions emphasized the importance of consulting the translation memory and glossary screenshots to understand the linguistic context and follow previously established decisions. My task was to ensure that the French-language content remained consistent with the project's existing linguistic references while respecting a specific brand requirement: the name U.S. Bank had to remain unchanged.

The Challenge

Accuracy beyond individual sentences

Financial content requires particular attention to terminology, consistency and clarity. Even when the exact document type is not available, the instructions for this assignment reveal two important requirements: alignment with existing linguistic resources and compliance with the client's naming conventions.

One of the central considerations was avoiding isolated editorial decisions that could conflict with established project formulations. A sentence may be grammatically correct on its own, yet still be inconsistent with the terminology or wording used elsewhere in a project. For this reason, contextual proofreading involves considering the linguistic references available alongside the text being reviewed.

Scope of Work

A focused linguistic revision

My assignment covered:

  • Contextual proofreading: Reviewing 1,500 words of Canadian French content translated from US English.
  • Translation memory consultation: Referring to the project's translation memory to take previously established formulations into account.
  • Glossary alignment: Consulting the project glossary to respect the terminology references provided.
  • Brand compliance: Preserving the name U.S. Bank in its original English form, without translation or adaptation.
  • Linguistic revision: Applying proofreading and revision principles while maintaining consistency with the available project references.

Working with Translation Resources

Using existing references as context

The project instructions specifically requested that I connect the translation memory and glossary screenshots to understand the assignment's context.

These resources served complementary purposes. The translation memory provided previously established linguistic formulations, while the glossary offered terminology references relevant to the project. Consulting both helped me approach the revision with a broader understanding of the existing linguistic framework rather than treating each expression independently.

An important distinction is that a translation memory and a glossary are not interchangeable. One provides translation references at the segment level, while the other supports terminology consistency. Both can contribute to informed editorial decisions, but they serve different functions.

Preserving Brand Identity

Keeping the name unchanged

A particularly explicit instruction concerned the client's name: U.S. Bank did not require translation.

Although this may appear to be a straightforward rule, it illustrates an essential aspect of professional localization and proofreading. Brand names, product names and other protected identifiers must follow the conventions established for the project.

Preserving the original name avoids unnecessary adaptations and maintains consistency in how the organization is identified throughout the content.

Key Takeaway

Professional proofreading extends beyond grammar and spelling.

Even within a relatively small 1,500-word mandate, linguistic quality depends on contextual awareness, appropriate use of project resources and respect for brand-specific requirements.

The objective is not simply to produce correct French, but to ensure that editorial decisions remain consistent with the broader linguistic framework of the project.
