Localizing an interactive vehicle-configuration experience from US English into Canadian French, with attention to automotive terminology, pricing information and interface constraints.

Project details

  • Language direction: US English → Canadian French
  • Scope: 50,284 words localized
  • Delivery: 3 batches
  • Client relationship: Ford assignment via TransPerfect

At a glance

A connected digital journey

Build & Price brings together vehicle filters, configuration choices, model specifications, starting prices and legal disclosures. The language across these elements needs to remain consistent while helping Canadian French-speaking users understand the information presented at each step.

The assignment covered 50,284 words delivered in three batches. The work required attention to terminology, readability and the way individual pieces of content fit into the wider interface.

The challenge

Keeping the experience coherent from screen to screen

The assignment involved a connected user journey rather than a collection of unrelated strings. Wording and terminology had to remain consistent across vehicle filters, specifications, pricing content and legal disclosures, while reading naturally for a Canadian French audience.

Key considerations included:

  • Maintaining consistent automotive terminology across related interface elements.
  • Presenting vehicle information and starting prices in clear, commercially appropriate language.
  • Working within the space constraints of pricing-disclosure modals.
  • Maintaining continuity across three separate delivery batches.

Scope of work

Localization with the interface in mind

I localized the interactive Ford Build & Price experience from US English into Canadian French. The work focused on automotive terminology, clear and natural wording, and consistency across the interface as a whole.

The main content areas included:

  • Vehicle filters and configuration choices.
  • Model specifications and starting-price content.
  • Legal disclosures, including content displayed in space-constrained modals.
  • Terminology and phrasing across the wider configuration journey.

What the assignment required

This project called for more than sentence-level accuracy. Terminology, commercial readability, interface space and continuity between related elements all had to be considered together. The aim was to produce Canadian French content that fit the interface and read as part of a coherent user journey, rather than as a series of separately translated fragments.
